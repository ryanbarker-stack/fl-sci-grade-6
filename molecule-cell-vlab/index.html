Finished your first rules? Open the science reference.

Carbohydrates. Sugars and starches are often used for energy; cellulose provides support. Many sugar names end in -ose. Ring units can help, but other groups have rings too.

Lipids. Fats store energy; phospholipids help form membranes. Long C/H-rich tails often help you recognize them. Some lipids, such as cholesterol, have joined rings instead.

Proteins. Built from amino acids; they do many jobs. Protein enzymes speed reactions. A folded chain may be a clue, but proteins do not all share one shape.

Nucleic acids. DNA and RNA are built from nucleotides. They store or help use genetic information. DNA’s double helix is a clue, but RNA does not usually look like a double helix.

Warning for every guide: one clue can fail. Use two clues when possible. Atom letters are more reliable than screen colors.

Many molecules form membranes, proteins, genetic material, and other cell structures. A cell contains proteins; a protein does not contain cells.

Conceptual scale change; sizes are not proportional. Organelles are cell structures, not an extra required rung in the hierarchy below.

atom → molecule → cell → tissue → organ → organ system → organism

Seven core cell structures • quick reference
Cell membrane
Boundary that regulates movement of materials.
Cell wall
Rigid support outside the membrane in cells that have one.
Nucleus
Contains most DNA in eukaryotic cells. Genetic instructions help direct activity; it is not a literal brain.
Cytoplasm
Material filling much of the cell, surrounding structures; many processes occur here.
Chloroplast
Captures light energy for photosynthesis in photosynthetic plant cells.
Mitochondrion / mitochondria
Helps release usable chemical energy from food and produce ATP. Energy is transformed, not made from nothing.
Vacuole
Stores materials. A large central vacuole also supports many plant cells.

Supporting parts: ribosomes build proteins; rough ER helps make/process proteins; smooth ER makes lipids and processes chemicals; Golgi sorts/packages products; lysosomes break down materials. You do not need to memorize these extra parts today.
